What Is a Torrent Settlement Demand Letter?

A torrent settlement demand letter is a legal notice sent by a copyright owner or their representative alleging that your internet connection was used to illegally download or share copyrighted material via peer-to-peer (P2P) networks. This article explains what these demand letters are, how copyright holders monitor torrent swarms to capture IP addresses, why account holders receive these notices, and what options recipients typically face.

Understanding the Settlement Demand Letter

A settlement demand letter is a formal document notifying an individual that they are suspected of copyright infringement. Instead of immediately initiating a full federal lawsuit, the copyright owner offers to resolve the claim out of court if the recipient pays a specified monetary settlement—often ranging from hundreds to thousands of dollars—within a designated timeframe. The letter typically outlines the title of the infringed work, the date and time of the alleged violation, and a warning of potential statutory damages if the matter proceeds to court.

How IP Addresses Are Tracked in Torrent Swarms

BitTorrent technology works by breaking large files into pieces distributed among a network of users, known as a "swarm." For users to upload and download pieces from one another, their internet protocol (IP) addresses must be visible to everyone participating in that swarm.

Copyright enforcement agencies and specialized monitoring firms join these public torrent swarms just like regular users. They log the public IP addresses actively downloading or seeding files containing their clients' copyrighted content, along with precise timestamps.

Why Demand Letters Are Sent to IP Addresses

Because an IP address only identifies an internet connection rather than a specific individual, copyright holders must take legal steps to reach the person paying for the service:

  1. Filing a "John Doe" Lawsuit: Copyright owners file a preliminary lawsuit against unknown defendants identified only by their IP addresses.
  2. Subpoenaing the ISP: The court grants a subpoena requiring the relevant Internet Service Provider (ISP) to disclose the name and physical address linked to that specific IP address at the logged timestamp.
  3. Mailing the Demand: Once the ISP complies, the copyright holder or their attorney sends the settlement demand letter directly to the account holder.

Motivation Behind the Letters

Copyright holders utilize settlement demand letters primarily as a cost-effective enforcement mechanism. Litigating copyright infringement cases through a full trial is expensive and time-consuming. By demanding settlements, copyright owners can recover damages, cover enforcement costs, and deter unauthorized distribution with minimal courtroom involvement.

Receiving a settlement demand letter indicates that an internet connection under your name was identified in a torrent swarm, but it is important to remember that an IP address alone does not conclusively prove who within a household or network committed the alleged infringement.
